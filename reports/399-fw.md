- Date 2026-11-13, boot64 at 19:02:47 UTC, capture 634 s after boot64 (the last dbg line came at 19:03:24, so over 9 minutes of waiting after it). No person, no card write. Stable restored (`stable up 19:14:11`).

## Result

**rpi-firmwared stops right after it writes its first mailbox request.** It writes `0xc1d66008` and then prints nothing more, not even after its own 1 s deadline. The VPU never sees the request: `mbox: alive` stays at **9 requests** (all from U-Boot) with both status words `40000000` (empty) up to 600 s.

### All `rpi-firmwared dbg:` lines (all at 19:03:24)

```
rpi-firmwared dbg: start
rpi-firmwared dbg: init done
rpi-firmwared dbg: dtb 36864 bytes
rpi-firmwared dbg: mailbox phys 0x3f00b880
rpi-firmwared dbg: registers mapped at 0x15880
rpi-firmwared dbg: buffer allocated
rpi-firmwared dbg: call tag 0x00030007, buffer at 0x1d66000
rpi-firmwared dbg: buffer filled, reading status
rpi-firmwared dbg: status 0x00000000, deadline in 1 s
rpi-firmwared dbg: wrote 0xc1d66008
```

**Last line before the boot goes silent:** `rpi-firmwared dbg: wrote 0xc1d66008`.
Only the first call (tag 0x00030007) was reached; there is no line for calls 2–4 and no "governor done".
Before it: `Starting Raspberry Pi firmware: temperature, clocks, board data (rpi-firmwared)`.
After it: only `mbox: alive` lines from the VPU, no kernel or init line, no panic, no exception.

### `mbox: alive` lines

60 lines, every 10 s from 10 s to 600 s, all the same apart from the time:
`mbox: alive N s, 9 requests, ARM->VPU 40000000 VPU->ARM 40000000`
(first at 19:02:57 = 10 s, the one after the write at 19:03:27 = 40 s, last at 19:12:47 = 600 s).

### What this says (for the firmware session)

- Status read before the write was `0x00000000` (not full), so the ARM saw a free mailbox and wrote. Yet the VPU's view of ARM->VPU stays `40000000` (empty) and its request counter does not move. So the write did not reach the register the VPU watches: possibly a different mailbox/offset than the one the VPU polls (phys 0x3f00b880 mapped at 0x15880; which offset was written is not in the line), or the mapping is cached/write-buffered.
- The 1 s deadline never ends the wait: there is no line after `wrote`, so the wait loop does not time out (the timer read in the loop, or the loop itself, is stuck).
