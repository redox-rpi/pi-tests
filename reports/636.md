**How CPU was measured:** this image has **no `cputop`** (`ion: command not found: cputop`; a first try at 00:13–00:23 gave no numbers for that reason, [try1/](636/try1/)). So I took `ps` twice, about 10 s apart, and divided the growth of each process's `ACTTIME` (summed over its threads) by the time between. The numbers are % of one core (100 % = one core busy).

## What to read from it

- **Step 3 probably did not drag the terminal.** Orbital used only 1.9 % (A) and 3.1 % (B) while `pointer-bench` reported 1800 moves "dragging" around the screen centre, where the terminal was (see term.png). Earlier rows saw a window drag cost "much of a core". I took no screenshot during the drag, so I cannot tell whether the window moved.
- With `gles-demo` running (step 4), Orbital is at about 55 % and gles-demo at about 37 %, with v3dd at about 4 %. That is mostly copying the demo's frames into its window (gles-demo's own split in Test 634: GL 1.2 ms, window 1.5 ms per frame), plus any drag.
- 1080p (run B) costs about the same as 640x480 here.
- **Baseline:** even at idle on the console, `usbhidd` uses 14–19 % and `xhcid` 6–9 % of a core. Together that is about a quarter of a core, with the USB mouse and the keyboard plugged in. During the drags it rises to about 22 % and 11 %. This may belong to #710/#711 (interrupt polling of the HID devices).
- `pointer-bench` itself: 0.4–0.5 %.
